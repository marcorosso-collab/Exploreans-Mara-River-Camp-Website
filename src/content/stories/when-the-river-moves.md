Every year, from July to October, the Great Migration reaches the Mara River. Wildebeest and zebra gather on the far bank in their thousands, and the crossing points close to Mara River Camp become the place everyone is watching.

## Waiting on the bank

A crossing day starts early. Your guide reads the herds the way a sailor reads weather: where they bunch, how they move, which way the wind is blowing. The vehicle settles at a respectful distance from the bank, and the waiting begins.

It can take an hour. It can take a morning. The herd edges forward, pulls back, edges forward again. Then one animal steps into the water, and the whole river moves.

## What stays with you

The sound arrives first: hooves on the bank, the rush of brown water, calls carrying from one side of the river to the other. Then the stillness afterwards, when the far bank is empty and the dust settles over the plains.

## Planning for the season

The herds follow the rain, so the timing of the crossings shifts from year to year. The camp sits close to several crossing points, and a short drive brings you to the bank. The camp team can advise on timing when you book.
